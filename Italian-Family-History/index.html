Belfiore & Circelli

The Belfiore line and the Circelli line

This site follows the paternal Belfiore line and the maternal Circelli line from the records that started the search: where they lived, what they did, and how everyone is connected.

How this started

It started with Sammy Belfore's obituary in The Standard-Star of New Rochelle, and with a few other family documents. The obituary named all nine children of Anthony and Maria Belfiore, four brothers and five sisters, and said that three of the brothers had become professional golfers, among the first native-born Americans to take up the sport, at a time when most of the professionals were still foreign-born.

Each document led to another. Ellis Island passenger lists, the census, and the Italian civil registers pointed to a small town in Campania, San Bartolomeo in Galdo. Michael Belfiore's death certificate, from 1952, named his parents as Anthony Belfiore and Maria Circelli. The town's marriage act then gave the names the American records had shortened. The husband was Leonardantonio Belfiore, and he was a foundling: the son of padre ignoto and madre ignota, father unknown and mother unknown. A child left at a foundling home, a brefotrofio, was often placed on the Ruota dei Proietti, a turning wheel set into a wall. The institution baptized the child and gave a new surname. "Belfiore" was the name chosen for him. It means bel fiore, beautiful flower. It was not a surname passed down. Other foundling names include Esposito, Innocenti, Proietti and Diotallevi. The Belfiore line cannot be traced past Leonardantonio. The older records are on Maria Donata Circelli's side.

Over time it became a family tree from the Circelli parents in San Bartolomeo to Enzo, a page for the crossing, a page for the three golfing brothers, and an archive of the records. The claims on this page link to those records, so you can look at the original for yourself.

Not every first reading held. The passenger list for the SS Oregon, 2 December 1896, names Maria Doreta Circelli, and it once looked as if she had crossed alone, a widow traveling under her maiden name. Italian women kept their birth surnames in civil and travel records, so Circelli on a list does not say whether her husband was living. Frank, born in New Rochelle in 1897, and Sammy, born there in 1899, both had a father named Anthony or Antonio. Michael's 1918 draft card says he became a citizen through his father's papers. The working identification is that Leonardantonio is the Anthony Belfiore who died in New Rochelle in 1938. It is strong, and it is not proven. His naturalization file at the National Archives would confirm it. See his profile on the family canvas.

Ellis Island passenger record: Circelli, Michele, arriving from Naples in 1898. He traveled with Salvatore Circelli. A relationship to Maria Donata is probable, and it is not proven.

This research is still open. Where a reading is uncertain, the page says so. Leonardantonio's death certificate (number 42184) and Frances Towey's death certificate have been identified. The 1900 census, once the record that would show whether he came to America, is now in hand: see the update below.

Update (November 2026): The 1900 census has been found. Antonio, born September 1861, was at 13 Lawton Street in New Rochelle, already a citizen, and he told the census taker he had come in 1890. Maria Donata's own birth act, written on 25 September 1863, names her mother Rita Mansolino. "Marianna Mita" stays as the name the marriage record first seemed to give. 1900 census · birth act

The two lines

On the family tree the paternal line is the Belfiores and the maternal line is the Circellis. The two lines meet at the marriage in San Bartolomeo in Galdo in 1884.

The paternal line

Belfiore

The Belfiore name begins with Leonardantonio. He was a foundling, so there is no father or mother to follow past him. On 27 January 1884 he married Maria Donata Circelli in San Bartolomeo in Galdo. A Leonardo Belfiore of twenty-nine landed in New York on the Hindoustan on 24 December 1890, from San Bartolomeo. By 1900 he was Antonio, a citizen, at 13 Lawton Street in New Rochelle, and he told the census taker he had come in 1890.

The working identification, not yet proven, is that he is the Anthony Belfiore who died in New Rochelle on 11 July 1938. The nine children are the next generation of the line: brothers Michael, Frank, Joseph and Sammy, and sisters Rose, Caroline, Mollie (Mrs. Anthony Drew), Phyl (Mrs. Angelo Paternostro) and Mary (Mrs. Joseph D'Onofrio). Sammy, Frank and Joseph became professional golfers. Michael, the eldest brother and the only one born in Italy, was a house painter in New Rochelle, and the direct line runs through him.

The maternal line

Circelli and Mansolino

Maria Donata Circelli was born in San Bartolomeo in Galdo on 25 September 1863. Her father, Giuseppe Circelli, a farmer of thirty-six, presented her at the town hall that morning. Her mother was Rita Mansolino. The oldest record yet found on this line is a marriage promise of 17 January 1850 between Giuseppe Circelli and Anna Rita Mansolino. It names Giuseppe's parents, Donato Circelli and Vincenza, and Anna Rita's parents, Francesco Mansolino and Brigida. Vincenza's surname is probably Minichilli. The reading is uncertain. An earlier reading of the 1884 marriage act gave Maria Donata's mother as Marianna Mita, and that reading is kept beside the birth act.

In December 1896 Maria Donata crossed on the SS Oregon with three children: Michele, twelve, and two girls listed as Francesca, nine, and Anna, six. The censuses show the girls as Filomena, Phyl, and Mary. Phyl married Angelo Paternostro in 1906 and raised five children in Hartford. Mary married Joseph D'Onofrio in 1911 and raised six in New Jersey and Yonkers. All 52 indexed Circelli records on Italy's Antenati portal come from this one town. Michele and Salvatore Circelli, who arrived on the Alsatia in 1898, are probably kin. The relationship is not proven.

The records that started it

Some of the records behind the two lines, and what each one led to.

San Bartolomeo in Galdo, 25 September 1863

Maria Donata's own birth, 1863

On the 25th of September 1863, at ten in the morning, Giuseppe Circelli carried his new daughter to the town hall of San Bartolomeo in Galdo. He was a farmer of thirty-six. Her mother was Rita Mansolino. They named her Maria Donata, and she was baptized that same day.

Maria Donata Circelli's birth act, 25 September 1863, act 191. Her father Giuseppe presented her. Her mother was Rita Mansolino. Birth act 191

The SS Oregon, December 1896

The little girls on the Oregon

When Maria Donata crossed in December 1896 she had three children with her: Michele, twelve, and two little girls listed as Francesca, nine, and Anna, six. The censuses show who they were: Filomena, our Phyl, and Mary. Phyl married Angelo Paternostro in 1906 and raised five children in Hartford. Mary married Joseph D'Onofrio in 1911 and raised six in New Jersey and Yonkers.

New York, 24 December 1890

Leonardantonio came first, in 1890

Six years before his family, a Leonardo Belfiore of twenty-nine landed in New York on the Hindoustan, two days before Christmas 1890. By 1900 he was Antonio, a citizen of New Rochelle, who told the census taker he had come in 1890.

San Bartolomeo in Galdo, 1884

The marriage record: Leonardantonio was a foundling

A translated marriage record from the Comune di San Bartolomeo in Galdo confirms the marriage of Leonardantonio Belfiore to Maria Donata Circelli on 27 January 1884. The name in the American records, Anthony, is the compound given name shortened by clerks. Leonardantonio was a foundling, the son of padre ignoto and madre ignota, both parents unknown. "Belfiore" was not an inherited surname. It was assigned by a foundling home: bel fiore, beautiful flower. The Belfiore line cannot be traced past Leonardantonio himself. The marriage act names Maria Donata's parents as Giuseppe Circelli and Marianna Mita. Her birth act, found later, names her mother Rita Mansolino. Both readings are kept.

Atti di matrimonio: the 1884 marriage record from San Bartolomeo in Galdo that showed Leonardantonio was a foundling. Marriage act

Ellis Island, 2 December 1896

Maria Doreta Circelli, found on the SS Oregon

The Ellis Island Foundation passenger database has her record: "Circelli, Maria Doreta," arrived 2 December 1896, from Genoa and Naples. She is listed under her birth surname, Circelli, which is what Italian civil and travel records did. An earlier reading took that to mean she was a widow, crossing without her husband, and that Leonardantonio had died in Italy before 1896.

Update (2026): This widow theory is no longer the working view. See the note above and Leonardantonio's profile on the family canvas.

Update (November 2026): She did not cross alone. Michele, twelve, and her daughters Filomena and Mary (written Francesca and Anna on the list) were with her.

Ellis Island passenger record: "Circelli, Maria Doreta", SS Oregon, arrived 2 December 1896.

Benevento, Campania

San Bartolomeo in Galdo, the ancestral town

All 52 indexed Circelli records on Italy's Antenati civil records portal originate from a single town: San Bartolomeo in Galdo, province of Benevento, Campania. The surname appears nowhere else in Italy's digitized records. Ellis Island manifests agree: every Circelli passenger lists a variation of the town name. The nearby town of Circello, about 15 km away, whose patron saint is San Michele, almost certainly gave the surname its origin. The family named their eldest son Michele.

SS Alsatia manifest (1898): Michele and Salvatore Circelli traveling together from "S.Dartolomeo in Calor" (San Bartolomeo in Galdo). Alsatia list

Sammy's obituary, The Standard-Star

The nine children

Sammy Belfore's obituary in The Standard-Star (New Rochelle) named every sibling: brothers Michael, Frank and Joseph; sisters Rose, Caroline, Mollie (Mrs. Anthony Drew), Phyl (Mrs. Angelo Paternostro) and Mary (Mrs. Joseph D'Onofrio). It confirmed Sammy was born on Christmas Day 1899, that he changed the spelling to "Belfore," and that three of the four brothers were professional golfers, with Michael described as "their non-golfing brother." The obituary records the mother's maiden name as "Cicelli," a variant spelling of Circelli. In 1910 Maria Donata herself told the census taker she had borne nine children and that all nine were living. The censuses suggest that the "Lena" of the early records grew up to be Carolyn.

Professional golf, 1920s to 1970s

The golfing brothers

Three sons of Italian immigrants, Sammy, Frank and Joseph Belfiore, became professional golfers at a time when the sport was almost exclusively dominated by foreign-born, usually Scottish, professionals. Sammy served at Ridgeview Country Club in Westchester, won a PGA Tour event, and coached Clinton Russell, a prominent blind golfer whose story was covered by Time magazine. He later became head pro at Seabreeze in Daytona Beach, where brother Frank ran the pro shop. Joseph became head golf professional at the Country Club of Detroit in Grosse Pointe Farms. He won the Michigan PGA Championship twice, in 1933 and 1937, served as Michigan PGA President from 1959 to 1960, competed in the 1927 Metropolitan Open alongside Walter Hagen and Gene Sarazen, and wrote a book called Golfing Aids (1940). He died in 1963, leaving two sons: David Belfore and Joseph F. "Jeffery" Belfore Jr. Sammy married Virginia Hupp, connected to the Hupp Motor Car Company of Detroit.

The Irish connection

Frances Towey, Michael's second wife

Michael Belfiore was married twice. His first wife, whose name is not known, was the mother of his daughter Dorothy. After that marriage he married Frances Towey, an Irish-American woman whose surname traces to County Mayo and County Roscommon in western Ireland. With Frances he had two sons: Edward ("Eddie") and Joseph (Seb's grandfather). Irish and Italian Catholic marriages were rare before the Second World War.

Michael Belfiore's WWII draft registration card, 1942, by which time he was married to Frances Towey.

January 2026, Tampa, Florida

Enzo Belfiore, the sixth generation

Enzo Belfiore, born January 2026 in Tampa, Florida, is the sixth generation from the immigrants. He holds US, UK and German citizenship. He carries a name that began as a foundling's surname in the Apennine mountains of southern Italy.

One descent

This is the line through Michael, from San Bartolomeo in Galdo to Davis Island in Tampa. The Circelli kin, the sisters and the golfing brothers sit beside it on the tree.

Generation 1. The immigrants

Leonardantonio Belfiore & Maria Donata Circelli

Married 27 January 1884 in San Bartolomeo in Galdo, Benevento, Italy. Leonardantonio was a foundling. "Belfiore" was assigned by a foundling home, not inherited. Maria's parents were Giuseppe Circelli and, on the first reading of the marriage act, Marianna Mita. An earlier reading of the evidence had Leonardantonio dying in Italy before 1896, and Maria arriving alone on the SS Oregon on 2 December 1896 under her birth surname. Nine children were born. An "Anthony Belfiore" died on 11 July 1938 in New Rochelle. Whether that man was Leonardantonio was, for a time, unresolved.

Update (November 2026): Maria Donata was born on 25 September 1863. Her parents, Giuseppe Circelli and Anna Rita Mansolino, married in 1850. The name Marianna Mita, in the paragraph above, stays as the earlier reading. Leonardantonio did come: a Leonardo Belfiore of twenty-nine is on the Hindoustan on 24 December 1890, and Antonio is at 13 Lawton Street in the 1900 census. The widow reading stays above, as the way the story first looked.

Circelli birth record from San Bartolomeo in Galdo: Antonio Maria Circelli, 1862. A different branch of Maria Donata's Circelli family. Birth act

Generation 2. The non-golfing brother

Michael Kirtus Belfiore

Born 15 December 1883 or 1885, in Italy. The 1918 draft card says 1885 and the 1942 draft card says 1883, a difference typical of the era. He arrived as a child and worked as a house painter in New Rochelle. The 1940 census gives his household: Michael (head, age 55, painter), Frances (wife), Edward (son) and Joseph (son). He married twice. His first wife, whose name is not known, was the mother of his daughter Dorothy. He then married Frances Towey, an Irish-American woman born in 1897, and with her had two sons, Edward and Joseph. He died on 16 January 1952 at New Rochelle Hospital. He was the only brother who did not become a golfer.

Generation 3. Seb's grandfather

Joseph Belfiore & Dorothy Belfiore

Joseph was Michael's son with Frances Towey, named after his uncle Joseph, the golfer, following Italian tradition. He married Dorothy Belfiore (née Hennessey), an Irish-American woman. His Social Security number was issued in Maryland. A Social Security Death Index entry places a death in West Babylon, New York.

Update (2026): The family places Joseph's death in Valrico, Florida, in 1986. The West Babylon place comes from a Social Security Death Index entry that has not been confirmed as his. The 1940 census gives his age as 17, so he was born around 1922 or 1923.

Update (November 2026): The Valrico memory stays, as family tradition. Beside it, the New Jersey death index lists Joseph C. Belfiore on 19 October 1994 in Princeton, age 71, born 1 December 1922. Both accounts are kept until a certificate is in hand. New Jersey death index
